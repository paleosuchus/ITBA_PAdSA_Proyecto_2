PART I: MATHEMATICAL CRITIQUE OF NAIVE TEXTBOOK RLS FAILURE MODES
================================================================================

When conventional textbook exponentially weighted Recursive Least Squares (RLS)
was applied to isolated 8-second windows (N = 1000 samples at fs = 125 Hz), the
Bayesian TPE optimizer pinned the hyperparameters against the hard boundaries of
the parameter space (lambda -> 0.9999, delta -> 0.01 with P[-1] = delta^{-1} I),
and the test set outlier fraction (26.58%) and median absolute error (1.85 BPM)
slightly trailed Project 1's Variable Step-Size LMS (VS-LMS: 25.98% outliers,
1.52 BPM MedAE).

Four coupled mathematical mechanisms explain this exact empirical behavior:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Extreme Spectral Oversampling & Toeplitz Collinearity of R_a
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All channels are pre-filtered to the physiological cardiac band [0.5, 4.0] Hz at
fs = 125 Hz (Nyquist frequency f_Nyq = 62.5 Hz). Consequently, the reference
accelerometer signals occupy only:

    B_rel = (4.0 - 0.5) / 62.5 = 5.6% of the Nyquist bandwidth

and are oversampled by a factor of f_Nyq / f_max = 15.625x. For a unit-stride
tapped-delay vector a_k[n] = [a_k[n], a_k[n-1], ..., a_k[n-M+1]^T with sample
spacing dt = 1/fs = 8 ms and M = 15 taps:
  - Adjacent samples exhibit lag-1 autocorrelation:
        r_aa(1) = E[a_k[n] a_k[n-1]] ~= 1 - 0.5 * (2*pi*f_rms / fs)^2 > 0.985
  - By Szego's theorem and Slepian's prolate spheroidal wave theory, the MxM
    autocorrelation matrix R_{a,k} of a signal bandlimited to W = 4/125 = 0.032
    cycles/sample has an effective numerical rank of only:
        rank_eff(R_{a,k}) ~= ceil(2 * W * M) + 1 ~= 2 to 3 eigenvalues per axis!
  - Out of L = 3M = 45 total MISO taps, only ~6 to 9 principal Slepian modes
    carry true physical motion energy, while the remaining ~36 to 39 eigenvalues
    lie in the numerical null-space (10^{-7} to 10^{-11}), yielding an extreme
    condition number:
        chi(R_a) = lambda_max(R_a) / lambda_min(R_a) >> 10^6.
  - Worse yet, with unit delay stride D = 1 and M = 15, the total temporal
    aperture of the filter is only:
        T_aperture = (M - 1) / fs = 14 / 125 = 0.112 seconds (112 ms).
    Because a 1.0 - 2.0 Hz running cadence has a period of 500 - 1000 ms, a
    quarter-wavelength (90-degree quadrature phase shift required to rotate the
    reference phase to match the optical PPG artifact) requires 125 - 250 ms!
    Thus, unit-stride M = 15 simultaneously suffers from massive collinear
    over-parameterization at high frequencies and insufficient temporal aperture
    at low frequencies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Why the Optimizer Pinned lambda -> 0.9999 (Dual Pathology of Exponential RLS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In standard exponentially weighted RLS, the sample correlation matrix is:

    Phi[n] = sum_{i=0}^{n} lambda^{n-i} a[i] a^T[i] + delta * lambda^{n+1} I_L

When lambda < 1 (e.g., lambda = 0.98, effective memory N_eff = 1/(1-lambda) = 50
samples = 0.4 s), two catastrophic phenomena occur simultaneously:

  (a) Exponential Decay of Tikhonov Regularization & Null-Space Blowup of P[n]:
      The regularizing diagonal term delta * lambda^{n+1} I_L decays to zero at
      rate lambda^n (at n = 300, 0.98^{300} = 0.0023). Along any eigenvector v_q
      in the 36-dimensional collinear null-space of R_a where a^T[n] v_q ~= 0,
      the Woodbury update:
          P[n+1] = lambda^{-1} (P[n] - k[n] a^T[n] P[n])
      multiplies the null-space eigenvalue by lambda^{-1} > 1 on every sample:
          v_q^T P[n] v_q ~= delta^{-1} * lambda^{-n} -> infinity!
      This exponential inflation of P[n] in the unexcited subspace amplifies
      numerical roundoff and out-of-subspace noise into huge tap-weight drift
      ||w[n]||^2, whereas first-order NLMS/VS-LMS has no inverse covariance
      matrix P[n] and naturally leaves unexcited null-space modes untouched.

  (b) Quasi-Periodic Cardiac Signal Cancellation (Over-Fitting the Heartbeat):
      The primary PPG input is d[n] = s[n] + m[n], where the target cardiac
      signal s[n] is NOT white zero-mean noise relative to a short horizon
      N_eff, but a narrowband quasi-periodic waveform in the exact same
      [0.5, 4.0] Hz band as a[n]. The finite-horizon empirical cross-correlation
      between the uncorrelated heartbeat s[n] and accelerometer a[n]:
          r_hat_{sa}[n] = (1 - lambda) sum_{i=0}^{n} lambda^{n-i} s[i] a[i]
      has non-zero finite-sample variance scaling as O(L / N_eff) = O(L(1 - lambda)).
      When N_eff is small (lambda < 0.995) and L = 3M = 45 is large, RLS uses
      its 45 degrees of freedom to project s[n] onto the collinear subspace of
      a[n], actively canceling the cardiac waveform itself!
      To prevent both null-space explosion of P[n] and cardiac signal leakage,
      TPE is forced to drive lambda -> 1.0 (hitting the ceiling 0.9999).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Why the Optimizer Pinned delta -> 0.01 (The Bias vs. Cold-Start Dilemma)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Once lambda is pinned to 0.9999 (so lambda^n ~= 1 across all N = 1000 samples),
the initial diagonal loading delta * I_L NEVER decays during the 8-second window:

    w[n] = ( sum_{i=0}^{n} a[i] a^T[i] + delta * I_L )^{-1} ( sum_{i=0}^{n} a[i] d[i] )

  - Furthermore, in naive RLS, delta is unscaled with respect to filter length
    L = 3M. Because each channel is unit-variance normalized, the instantaneous
    regressor energy is E[||a[n]||^2] = 3M = L.
  - If delta is large (e.g., delta = 10 to 100), the permanent ridge penalty
    delta * I_L heavily biases w[n] toward zero across a substantial fraction of
    the 1000-sample window, leaving motion artifacts uncancelled.
  - To eliminate this permanent shrinkage bias on the 6-9 dominant motion modes,
    TPE drives delta to the lower floor (delta -> 0.01, so P[-1] = 100 I_L).
  - However, setting P[-1] = 100 I_L with w[-1] = 0 creates two severe penalties:
      (i)  Underdetermined Early-Sample Over-Fitting (n < L): For the first L
           samples, rank(sum_{i=0}^n a[i] a^T[i]) <= n + 1 < L. With delta = 0.01,
           RLS acts as an minimum-norm interpolator that forces d[n] - w^T[n+1] a[n] ~= 0,
           wiping out the cardiac signal s[n] on early samples while producing
           huge a priori innovation spikes e[n] = d[n] - w^T[n] a[n] whenever
           the signal trajectory excites a new subspace direction.
      (ii) Unregularized Collinear Modes: With delta = 0.01, the ~36 collinear
           modes of R_a with small non-zero eigenvalues (10^{-3} to 10^{-1}) are
           inverted without sufficient Tikhonov damping, inflating excess
           mean-square error (EMSE) and elevating MedAE from 1.52 to 1.85 BPM.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Cold-Start Spectral Corruption & Quadratic Sensitivity to Impulsive Outliers
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Cold-Start Transients in Isolated Windows: Because w[-1] = 0 is reset at the
    start of every 8-second window (hard memoryless constraint across windows),
    the early samples n in [0, 2L] contain adaptation transients where w[n] is
    transitioning from 0 to w_*. Even with a Hann/Hamming taper, large early
    innovation spikes inject broadband spectral leakage into the zero-padded
    periodogram.
  - Quadratic Vulnerability to Impulsive Sensor Motion (e.g., Window #481):
    Standard RLS minimizes an l_2 quadratic loss, making the weight update
    w[n+1] = w[n] + k[n] e[n] unboundedly linear in the innovation e[n]. When a
    sudden physical impact or optical sensor detachment causes an impulsive
    spike in d[n], e[n] jumps by > 5 sigma, kicking w[n+1] far away from the
    true motion transfer function. Because P[n] has already decayed as O(1/n) by
    the middle of the window, the gain vector ||k[n]|| is small after the spike,
    so standard RLS takes hundreds of samples to recover from a single outlier!

PART II: ALGORITHMIC & STRUCTURAL INNOVATIONS IMPLEMENTED
================================================================================

To resolve all four failure modes while strictly respecting the cross-window
memoryless constraint, we re-architect the MISO RLS branch with five synergistic
mathematical innovations:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Dilated / Multi-Scale Tapped-Delay Reference Construction (`delay_step = D`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Instead of unit-sample spacing (dt = 8 ms), `build_U(acc, M, delay_step=D)`
constructs a dilated MISO regressor with tap spacing D in {1, 2, 3, 4} samples:

    a_k^{(D)}[n] = [ a_k[n], a_k[n - D], a_k[n - 2D], ..., a_k[n - (M - 1)D] ]^T

Mathematical benefits:
  - Decimating the tap spacing to D * dt (e.g., 16 ms, 24 ms, or 32 ms) respects
    the 4.0 Hz bandlimit (Nyquist interval = 125 ms) while breaking adjacent-tap
    collinearity, reducing the condition number chi(R_a) by 3 to 4 orders of
    magnitude.
  - Expands the physical impulse-response aperture by factor D:
        T_aperture = (M - 1) * D / fs
    reaching 250 - 540 ms (covering a full quarter- to half-cycle of low-frequency
    motion artifacts) with FEWER adaptive parameters M in [6, 18] (reducing
    cardiac over-fitting degrees of freedom and cutting O(L^2) complexity).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Dimension-Invariant Input-Power-Scaled Initialization (`delta`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Because each preprocessed accelerometer channel has unit variance, the expected
instantaneous norm of the L = 3M regressor vector is E[||a[n]||^2] = L. We
redefine the initial inverse covariance matrix as:

    P[0] = (delta * L)^{-1} I_L   <==>   Phi[0] = (delta * L) I_L

Now `delta` is strictly dimensionless and invariant to filter order M: delta = 1
corresponds to initial regularization energy equal to exactly one effective
regressor sample across all 3M taps.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Persistent Cyclic Diagonal Loading / Covariance Shrinkage (`diag_load`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To decouple the forgetting factor lambda from null-space covariance blowup, we
replace the standard rank-1 covariance update with a leaky, diagonally loaded
Riccati recursion:

    P_tilde[n+1] = lambda^{-1} ( P[n] - k[n] pi^T[n] )
    P[n+1]       = (1 - tau_leak) P_tilde[n+1] + tau_leak * P_0 * I_L

where P_0 = (delta * L)^{-1} and tau_leak = (1 - lambda) * diag_load.
  - Along any unexcited collinear/null-space eigenvector v_q where a^T[n] v_q = 0,
    the eigenvalue evolves according to the stable contraction:
        gamma_q[n+1] = (1 - tau_leak) lambda^{-1} gamma_q[n] + tau_leak * P_0
    which converges to a finite steady-state upper bound instead of exploding as
    lambda^{-n} -> infinity!
  - In the active motion subspace where a^T[n] v_p >> 0, the rank-1 Woodbury
    downdate dominates, preserving rapid Newton-type second-order tracking.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Huber M-Estimator Robust Innovation Clipping (`huber_c`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To immunize the RLS tap weights against impulsive optical artifacts and sudden
sensor impacts (such as Test Window #481), we replace the quadratic l_2 weight
update with a recursive Huber M-estimator update:
  - The cleared cardiac output signal remains the true a priori residual:
        e[n] = d[n] - w^T[n] a[n]
  - An exponentially weighted scale tracker estimates the innovation variance:
        sigma_e^2[n] = 0.98 * sigma_e^2[n-1] + 0.02 * e_tilde^2[n-1],  sigma_e^2[0] = 1
  - The weight vector update uses the Huber-bounded innovation:
        e_tilde[n] = clip( e[n], -huber_c * sigma_e[n], +huber_c * sigma_e[n] )
        w[n+1]     = w[n] + k[n] * e_tilde[n]
  This bounds the maximum influence function of any single sample on w[n+1],
  preventing mid-window motion shocks from destabilizing the converged filter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Strictly Intra-Window Backward-to-Forward Warm-Starting (`warm_start_ratio`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To eliminate the cold-start adaptation transient at n = 0, ..., 2L without
violating the cross-window memoryless constraint:
  - Within each isolated 1000-sample window, before running the forward pass from
    n = 0 to N - 1, we execute a reverse-time warm-up pass over the first
    N_warm = round(warm_start_ratio * N) samples of THAT SAME WINDOW from
    n = N_warm - 1 down to n = 0.
  - By the time the reverse pass reaches n = 0, the weight vector w_warm has
    already converged to the local motion transfer function at the start of the
    window, and the causal forward pass starts at n = 0 with w[0] = w_warm and
    re-conditioned covariance P[0] = 0.5 * P_warm + 0.5 * P_0 * I_L.
  - This completely removes the cold-start transient at the beginning of the
    window while remaining 100% memoryless across windows.

PART III: REPARAMETERIZED SEARCH TOPOLOGY & EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
================================================================================

To prevent Bayesian TPE boundary pinning, we reparameterize the RLS search space
in `optimization.py` (`CONDITIONAL_SEARCH_SPACE["rls_subspace"]`):

1. Effective Memory Horizon `N_eff` Instead of Linear `lambda`:
   - In standard RLS, sampling lambda uniformly in [0.96, 0.9999] places 75% of
     the prior probability mass in lambda in [0.96, 0.99], which corresponds to
     effective memory lengths N_eff = 1/(1 - lambda) of only 25 to 100 samples
     (0.2 s to 0.8 s, shorter than a single heartbeat!).
   - We instead parameterize `N_eff` on a log-uniform scale over [150.0, 2500.0]
     samples (1.2 s to 20.0 s) and map:
         lambda = 1.0 - (1.0 / N_eff)   in [0.99333, 0.99960].
   - This allocates uniform density per octave of effective memory length across
     the physically relevant multi-heartbeat regime.

2. Full Tunable RLS Subspace in `CONDITIONAL_SEARCH_SPACE`:
   - `M`                : ("int", 6, 18)              -> 18 to 54 total MISO taps
   - `delay_step`       : ("choice", [1, 2, 3, 4])    -> Tap dilation stride D
   - `N_eff`            : ("log_float", 150.0, 2500.0)-> Effective memory horizon
   - `delta`            : ("log_float", 0.05, 10.0)   -> Dimension-invariant ridge
   - `diag_load`        : ("log_float", 0.05, 1.0)    -> Null-space covariance floor
   - `huber_c`          : ("float", 1.5, 4.0)         -> Robust M-estimator clip
   - `warm_start_ratio` : ("choice", [0.0, 0.25, 0.5])-> Intra-window warm-up pass

3. Heart Rate Detector Selection Inside the Optimization Loop:
   - Continues to optimize `hr_algorithm` in {'periodogram', 'elgendi'} and its
     corresponding hyperparameters alongside the re-architected RLS filter via
     5-fold Subject-Wise Group K-Fold Cross-Validation.

PART IV: MODULE BREAKDOWN

Directory Structure:
--------------------
implementation/
├── __init__.py
├── dataset_preparation.py
├── preprocessing.py
├── reference_construction.py
├── LMS_algorithms.py
├── fusion.py
├── bpm_detection.py

├── optimization.py

└── code_structure_summary.txt

1. dataset_preparation.py
   - `load_ts(path)`: Loads 5D windows `(n_windows, 5, 1000)` and BPM labels `y`.
   - `split_participants(X)` & `get_participant_groups(X)`: Detects recording
     session / participant boundaries via 750-sample overlap matching.
   - `subject_wise_group_kfold(X, n_splits=5)`: Partitions windows strictly by
     subject ID using `sklearn.model_selection.GroupKFold` to prevent data leakage.

2. preprocessing.py
   - `bandpass(x, lo=0.5, hi=4.0, fs=125, order=4, causal=False)`: 4th-order
     zero-phase Butterworth bandpass filter in the cardiac band (30 - 240 BPM).
   - `normalize_amplitude(x)`: Zero-mean, unit-variance normalization per channel.
   - `preprocess_signal(x)`: Minimal preprocessing returning `(ppg1, ppg2, acc)`.

3. reference_construction.py
   - `build_U(acc, M, delay_step=1)`: Constructs the `(N, 3 * M)` MISO dilated
     tapped-delay regressor matrix with configurable tap stride `delay_step = D`.

4. LMS_algorithms.py
   - `rls(d, acc, M=12, lam=0.996, delta=1.0, delay_step=2, N_eff=None, diag_load=0.25, huber_c=2.5, warm_start_ratio=0.35, ...)`:
     Re-architected MISO RLS adaptive filter with all hyperparameters tunable,
     plus `_rls_dual_channel` for shared-covariance dual-PPG execution.
   - `nlms(d, acc, M=22, mu=0.01288, delta=0.5, w_init=None)`:
     Normalized MISO LMS configured with its optimal Project 1 hyperparameters.
   - `vs_lms(d, acc, M=18, mu_max=0.06558, mu_min=0.00791, alpha=0.98503, gamma=0.00053, delta=1.0, w_init=None)`:
     Kwong-Johnston Variable Step-Size MISO LMS configured with its optimal
     Project 1 hyperparameters.
   - `lms_bucket(ppg1, ppg2, acc, algorithm='rls', M=None, **hyperparams)`:
     Runs the selected adaptive filter (`rls`, `nlms`, or `vs_lms`) in parallel on
     `ppg1` and `ppg2` and fuses `e1` and `e2` via `snr_weighted_late_fusion`.

5. fusion.py
   - `estimate_cardiac_snr(sig)`: In-band cardiac spectral SNR in `[0.5, 4.0] Hz`.
   - `compute_snr_weights(e1, e2)` & `snr_weighted_late_fusion(e1, e2)`: Combines
     parallel-filtered PPG channels using SNR weights $w_i = \text{SNR}_i / (\text{SNR}_1 + \text{SNR}_2)$.

6. bpm_detection.py
   - `zero_padded_periodogram_bpm(x, fs=125, nfft=8192, lo=0.5, hi=4.0, window_type='hann')`:
     Direct zero-padded periodogram peak detector in `[0.5, 4.0] Hz`.
   - `elgendi_systolic_peak_bpm(x, fs=125, w1_sec, w2_sec, beta_offset)`:
     Time-domain systolic peak heart rate detector.
   - `estimate_bpm_window(e_window, hr_algorithm, **hr_params)`: HR bucket function.
   - `objective_function(bpm_estimated, bpm_true)`: Outlier-penalized error metric.

7. pipeline.py
   - `reconstruct_heart_signal(window_5d, ...)`: End-to-end window signal cleaning.
   - `predict_window_bpm(window_5d, ...)`: End-to-end single-window BPM prediction.
   - `HeartRateEstimatorModel`: Encapsulates a configured Adaptive Filter + HR
     pipeline with `.predict(X)`, `.predict_with_signals(X)`, and
     `.from_project1_optimal(algorithm)`.

8. optimization.py
   - `optimize_hr_pipeline(X, y, n_splits=5, ...)`: Optimizes ONLY the
     re-architected `rls` hyperparameters (`M`, `delay_step`, `N_eff`, `delta`,
     `diag_load`, `huber_c`, `warm_start_ratio`) and selects the best HR detection
     model (`periodogram` vs `elgendi`) using Subject-Wise Group K-Fold CV and
     2-phase Conditional TPE optimization with `MedianFoldPruner`.

9. pipeline_optimization_and_evaluation.ipynb
   - Executes the re-architected RLS pipeline, compares CV and Test Set
     performance of RLS against Project 1 optimal NLMS and VS-LMS and the
     unfiltered baseline, plots the spread of Test BPMs for RLS colored by
     subject, visualizes a single window transforming across each step of the RLS
     pipeline, compares a good RLS example against an RLS outlier example, and
     runs RLS on the exact same outlier window (#481) analyzed in Project 1.
